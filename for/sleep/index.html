which trials tested melatonin for insomnia in older adults, what did they measure, and does the meta-analysis agree?
Answer

The registry and the papers answer together. NCT00230737, Melatonin Randomized Trial for Insomnia in the Elderly: phase 2, completed, 27 enrolled, 0.4 mg against 4.0 mg, for sleep initiation and maintenance disorders. NCT07695246: cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia with and without adjunct melatonin in older adults, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, measuring sleep, circadian outcomes and daytime functioning. The largest paper, Wade and colleagues, nightly prolonged-release melatonin for six months: 791 randomized, 722 analysed, patients 55 and older, sleep latency shortened by 15.6 minutes in the 65 to 80 group (95 percent CI minus 25.3 to minus 6.0). The systematic review and meta-analysis of melatonin and ramelteon in older adults with chronic insomnia searched 1990 to June 2021, the year the Beers Criteria chose, and pools these designs. One hit was dropped on reading: the tasimelteon phase III trial tests a receptor agonist, not melatonin.

Evidence

2.NCT07695246 · ClinicalTrials.gov registration · CBT-I with adjuvant melatonin in older adults with chronic insomnia, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled
3.Wade et al. · PMC open access · nightly prolonged release melatonin for 6 months · "the observed effect on sleep latency for patients aged 65-80 years (-15.6 min; 95% CI -25.3 to -6.0)"
4.Use of Melatonin and/or Ramelteon for the Treatment of Insomnia in Older Adults · systematic review and meta-analysis · PMC open access · "searches were conducted from 1 January 1990 to 20 June 2021"; Beers Criteria as the start

Conflict. None found in the retrieved evidence.
Gap. The primary outcome measure of NCT00230737 is on the registration, which this answer cited by title and arms; the endpoint line was not read. Whether the meta-analysis pooled Wade's trial is a question for its tables, one read away.

Trial registrations.

Ask, and read the citation

Every answer names the document and the page it came from. The first question after a quiet spell can take half a minute while the collection wakes up; your assistant is told so and asks again by itself.

The door never sees your files, and cannot be changed by anyone, us included, without a new seal and a new date on this page. It keeps nothing you ask unless you choose to, with two boxes that start unticked when you connect: "tell me when a question can be answered later", which keeps only the questions the collection could not answer, and "keep my questions, without my name", which keeps the words for 180 days with no name, address or time of day beside them. You can change both on your start page.

What it will not pretend to know

Boundedness is the feature.

It is research literature, not medical advice. The door cites the paper, the page and the registration; what to do about it is not retrieval.

A near miss is read and dropped. A receptor agonist trial surfaced for a melatonin question; reading it put it out, and the answer says so.

The window is stated. A review's search dates are part of its citation, because a 2021 window cannot pool a 2026 trial.

The seal is the date. Nothing is added, changed or removed after the seal; a new seal means a new date on this page.
